title: "Health Insurance and Entrepreneurship Lock"
collection: publications

excerpt: >
  Entrepreneurship lock arises when limited access to health insurance discourages self-employment. I study whether this constraint binds for young adults by exploiting the Affordable Care Act dependent coverage mandate, which extended parental plan eligibility to age 26. In a difference-in-differences design, I find that the mandate raised self-employment by 26%. I then develop a mean-variance framework to separate the subsidy channel (lower expected premiums and out-of-pocket medical costs) from the risk-reduction channel (lower variance of total payments) and find that the risk-reduction channel is the primary mechanism. Finally, I examine distributional effects and find that women and the unhealthy benefit the most.
